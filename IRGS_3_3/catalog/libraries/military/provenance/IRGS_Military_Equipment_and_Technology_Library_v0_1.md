# IRGS — Military Equipment and Technology Library

**Version:** 0.1 — era and equipment-category index  
**Date:** 11 October 2026  
**Parent module:** Military Life Simulation Engine v0.4  
**Scope:** Equipment and technology families. Specific models, statistics, prices and performance profiles will be developed later.

This library uses six user-selected groups: **Antiquity, Early Centuries AD, Middle Ages, Early Modern, Contemporary, and Science Fiction.** It covers military equipment and the everyday tools and technologies that sustain military life.

Standardization here means consistent categories and naming within this module. Standardizing mechanics across the IRGS engines remains deferred.

## 1. Era groups

| Era ID | Group | Working scope |
|---|---|---|
| ERA-ANT | Antiquity | Ancient societies before the first century AD; bronze and iron technologies where locally established |
| ERA-EAD | Early Centuries AD | Roughly the first–fifth centuries AD; early imperial and late-antique contexts |
| ERA-MED | Middle Ages | Roughly AD 500–1500; later regional/period subdivisions can refine availability |
| ERA-EMD | Early Modern | Roughly AD 1500–1800; expanding gunpowder warfare, maritime systems and organized military supply |
| ERA-CON | Contemporary | Used broadly in this library for industrial and modern military contexts, approximately the nineteenth century to the present |
| ERA-SCI | Science Fiction | Fictional future or alternative technological contexts; availability determined by the setting |

These are catalogue boundaries, not universal historical period definitions. Early Centuries AD is separated from Antiquity for browsing even though it belongs to antiquity in many historical classifications. Contemporary is intentionally broad enough to hold industrial, world-war and recent equipment without adding more top-level eras.

Historical dates and technology adoption vary by region. A family listed under an era is a candidate for that era’s regional library, not equipment available to every person or institution. Older equipment may remain in use later. The science-fiction row lists possibilities, not a requirement that every setting contain them.

## 2. Standard equipment categories

Use the same categories throughout the six groups.

| Code | Category | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| WPN | Personal weapons | Melee weapons, projectile weapons, firearms and other personal weapon systems |
| PRO | Protection and clothing | Armor, shields, helmets, uniforms, footwear and protective gear |
| SUP | Support weapons | Siege equipment, artillery and other crew-operated weapon systems |
| MUN | Ammunition and expendables | Projectiles, propellants, cartridges, shells and setting-specific consumables |
| KIT | Personal and camp equipment | Carrying gear, bedding, shelter, cooking, lighting and hygiene equipment |
| TRN | Land transport and animal equipment | Saddlery, pack equipment, carts, wagons, vehicles and rail transport |
| NAV | Naval equipment | Boats, ships, rigging, propulsion and maritime support |
| AIR | Aviation and space equipment | Balloons, aircraft, spacecraft and associated equipment where applicable |
| COM | Communication and administration | Signals, correspondence, records, printing, telegraphy, radio and digital systems |
| OBS | Observation and navigation | Maps, surveying, optical equipment, sensors and navigation aids |
| ENG | Engineering and production | Hand tools, agriculture, construction, workshops, machinery and utilities |
| MED | Care and medical equipment | Medical supplies, care facilities, evacuation equipment and setting-specific treatment technology |

Vehicles and installations belong alongside portable items. Animal equipment is catalogued here; living animals remain actors in the game.

## 3. Military equipment by era

| Era | Personal weapons | Protection and clothing | Support weapons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Antiquity** | Spears, swords, axes, daggers, bows, slings and thrown weapons; bronze or iron construction according to context | Shields, helmets, textile protection, scale and other regional armor constructions; mail in appropriate later contexts | Siege engines and mechanical artillery in applicable societies; chariot weapon equipment where used |
| **Early Centuries AD** | Iron/steel blades, spears, lances, bows, slings, javelins and regional crossbows | Shields, helmets, mail, scale, lamellar and other region-specific body protection | Mechanical artillery, siege engines and assault equipment |
| **Middle Ages** | Swords, spears, lances, axes, maces, polearms, bows and crossbows; early firearms in appropriate later contexts | Shields, helmets, padded armor, mail, scale/lamellar, coats of plates, brigandines and later full plate where attested | Siege engines, siege towers and early gunpowder artillery where available |
| **Early Modern** | Pikes, swords, sabres, bayonets, muskets, pistols and rifles; matchlock, wheellock and flintlock families as applicable | Military clothing and increasingly organized uniforms; helmets, cuirasses and other armor for selected roles | Gunpowder cannon, mortars, siege artillery and naval guns |
| **Contemporary** | Percussion and cartridge firearms, repeating and automatic weapons, sidearms and infantry anti-armor systems, according to date | Field uniforms, camouflage, helmets, carrying systems, respirators and body armor appropriate to the period | Machine guns, mortars, artillery, rockets, missiles and remotely operated/unmanned weapon systems where fielded |
| **Science Fiction** | Advanced projectile, energy and other setting-defined weapons; conventional weapons can continue alongside them | Advanced body armor, powered armor, environmental suits and setting-defined active protection | Advanced artillery, directed-energy systems, orbital weapons and autonomous weapon platforms where the setting supports them |

This is a family index. An early-modern musket, industrial-era rifle and fictional energy weapon will eventually have distinct profiles; this edition assigns none.

## 4. Transport, communications and observation

| Era | Transport and platforms | Communication and administration | Observation and navigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Antiquity** | Pack/riding animals, carts, wagons, chariots and oared/sailing vessels where established | Messengers, oral instructions, standards, sound/visual signals and written records in literate societies | Local route knowledge, surveying, charts/maps and astronomical navigation in appropriate contexts |
| **Early Centuries AD** | Animal transport, wagons, cavalry equipment and merchant/war vessels | Courier networks, written orders, administrative records and military signals | Maps, surveying tools, coastal knowledge and astronomical navigation |
| **Middle Ages** | Saddlery, pack trains, carts, wagons, sailing ships and galleys | Messengers, correspondence, standards, horns, bells and written records; printing where locally established | Maps, compasses, surveying and maritime navigation equipment according to region/date |
| **Early Modern** | Animal trains, wagons, sailing warships and merchant vessels | Printed materials, courier/postal networks, signal flags and institutional records | Maps, compasses, surveying and optical instruments; improving maritime navigation systems |
| **Contemporary** | Railways, motor vehicles, armored platforms, ships, submarines, aircraft and helicopters according to date | Telegraph, telephone, radio, satellite and digital communication systems according to date | Photography, optical instruments, radar, sonar, night vision, thermal observation and satellite navigation where established |
| **Science Fiction** | Advanced vehicles, walkers/Mechs, spacecraft, orbital facilities and other setting-defined transport | Advanced networks, AI-assisted information systems and long-distance communications with setting-defined limits | Advanced sensors, autonomous reconnaissance, augmented displays and setting-specific navigation |

Fast communication is not automatically instantaneous communication. Science-fiction propulsion, artificial intelligence and sensors require setting definitions before they become capabilities in play.

## 5. Everyday equipment, tools and supporting technology

| Era | Personal, household and camp equipment | Engineering, production and supply | Care and medical equipment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Antiquity** | Clothing, bags, bedding, shelters, cooking vessels, storage containers, lamps and everyday hand tools | Agriculture, weaving, pottery, carpentry, metalworking, construction and water-management equipment appropriate to the society | Period-specific care supplies, instruments and casualty transport |
| **Early Centuries AD** | Clothing, tents, blankets, cookware, water containers, lighting and repair tools | Specialized craft workshops, mills, road/bridge equipment, fortifications and supply storage | Medical instruments, dressings, medicines and organized care equipment where present |
| **Middle Ages** | Clothing, camp shelters, bedding, cookware, lamps, containers, sewing and craft tools | Farming tools, smithies, mills, construction equipment, siege engineering and storage facilities | Period-specific surgical/care instruments, dressings, medicines and care facilities |
| **Early Modern** | Uniform/clothing supplies, tents, bedding, packs, cookware, lighting, cleaning and repair kits | Arsenals, foundries, powder mills, printing, shipyards, depots, hand workshops and powered mills | Medical chests, instruments, dressings and field/naval care equipment |
| **Contemporary** | Manufactured uniforms, personal kits, field shelters, preserved rations, portable lighting, hygiene supplies and later electronics | Steam/electrical industry, engines, machine tools, generators, mechanized engineering, refrigeration and digital diagnostics according to date | Field hospitals, ambulances, surgical equipment, medical supply systems and later advanced diagnostic/life-support equipment |
| **Science Fiction** | Advanced survival kits, environmental shelters, smart equipment and setting-defined food/water systems | Robotics, advanced fabrication, automated supply, specialized power systems and material production where supported | Advanced diagnostics, cybernetics, regenerative or other setting-defined medical technology |

Medical categories describe equipment families only. They do not grant a treatment capability or a recovery rule. Production categories likewise do not guarantee that an institution can manufacture every item it possesses.

## 6. Later expansion

Later entries follow:

**Era → region/setting and date → branch or occupation → equipment family → specific item.**

Preserve a family across eras when its role continues. Create a variant when construction, operating method or requirements actually differ. Era tags organize content; they do not issue equipment, grant competence or change campaign state.

No weapon models, armor statistics, prices, item weights, medical effects or technology-performance values are assigned in this edition.

## 7. Historical grounding and design basis

The six-group structure follows the user’s requested organization. Equipment families are broad editorial summaries, supported by representative sources rather than an exhaustive regional inventory.

- [British Museum — Britain and Europe, 800 BC–AD 43](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/galleries/britain-and-europe-800-bc-ad-43): regional transition between bronze and iron tools/weapons.
- [Metropolitan Museum of Art — Arms and Armor](https://www.metmuseum.org/departments/arms-and-armor): equipment categories and regional diversity.
- [Metropolitan Museum of Art — European Helmets, 1450–1650](https://www.metmuseum.org/exhibitions/listings/2000/european-helmets): changing protection and the expanding use of firearms.
- [National Army Museum — Late flintlock musket, c. 1838](https://collection.nam.ac.uk/detail.php?acc=1979-07-52--1): overlapping firearm technologies during industrial transitions.
- [National Army Museum — Weapons of the Western Front](https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/weapons-western-front): artillery, machine guns, aircraft, tanks and protective equipment.
- [RAF Museum — History of aviation timeline](https://www.rafmuseum.org.uk/research/research-enquiries/history-of-aviation-timeline/): twentieth-century aviation development.
- [US Army — Soldier equipment and modernization, 2020](https://www.army.mil/article/231403/ccdcs_road_map_to_modernizing_the_army_soldier_lethality): recent personal protection, electronics, navigation and power equipment.

Science-fiction categories are design options, not historical claims. The library uses the Military Life Simulation Engine v0.4 and the existing content-inheritance approach, without importing numerical fantasy profiles or standardizing new mechanics in the core.
