## A2. Visual identity and style

**Established direction:** follow the approved reference style unless the user requests another treatment. Preserve faces, body proportions, equipment silhouettes, heraldry and materials across images. Anime or other stylized treatments may change rendering language while retaining identifying features and design structure.

**Proposed production approach:** use readable silhouettes, tactile materials, believable weight, contact and task-appropriate wear. Let ordinary work, maintenance, relationships and service be as visually credible as combat. “Become a Legend” does not justify depicting unearned rank, royal status, supernatural glow or guaranteed victory.

The v0.2 restrained-realism preference is optional and subordinate to the approved references or requested style. It cannot exclude an authorized anime/stylized treatment. No universal grime, damage, idealized anatomy or heroic expression should replace the approved individual appearance.

## A3. Palette, materials and typography

Approved character/equipment colors and heraldry take precedence over a general page palette. In particular, preserve the shield leather's red and yellow/gold arrangement. A decorative muted palette must not silently recolor it.

The earlier proposed page tokens remain optional: ink `#252724`, warm paper `#E8DFC9`, stone `#8B8D82`, worn leather `#77543A`, wool green `#59634C`, muted red `#873F36`, steel `#969FA1`. They are interface/background suggestions, not exact approved heraldic colors, historical dye claims or gameplay signals.

Distinguish mail, leather, cloth, wood and steel materially. Show believable layering, fastenings, movement and wear, using the depicted scene's actual condition. A repair changes appearance only after the relevant game event or in a clearly labelled proposed design view. Legendary importance does not imply supernatural visual effects.

Proposed text treatment: readable display type for short titles and clear body labels, concise annotations and adequate spacing. Avoid pseudo-Gaelic filler for actual inscriptions. Select font licensing, export size and contrast for the delivery medium. Test legibility at final scale; color must not be the only status indicator.

## A6. Coopian shield—accepted design draft

The current supplied draft is accepted **as a design draft**, not as completed or proven equipment. Preserve:

1. Curved heater shield enlarged to suit Ian's size.
2. Large opposing relief cuts inspired by the supplied historical shield forms.
3. Deeper relief on the sword side and shallower opposing relief.
4. Substantial protection retained above and below both cuts.
5. **Exactly five horizontal steel bands across the front.**
6. **Heraldry painted directly onto the steel bands**, maintaining the intended design across the face.
7. **Two traditional shield arm straps plus one smaller strap connecting to the over-bracer harness; quick-release clasp as corrected by S14.**
8. **Handle on the sword side, beside the deeper relief.**
9. Consistent padding, bracer connections, fastenings and release arrangements across all views.

Track sword side relative to the wearer. Apparent left/right changes between front and rear views; label wearer-relative orientation in production notes. Do not infer Ian's handedness from a mirrored image. Detached harness/bracer studies must match the main shield's actual connection points and orientation.

Experts in the game may identify necessary changes through construction, fitting and testing. Record those proposed/accepted revisions and actual work when they occur; do not silently “engineer away” accepted features or declare the draft tested by showing a convincing image. No drawing establishes mass, protection, durability, release time or handling advantage.

This design uses paint on steel bands. It is not the old detachable heraldic leather automatically transferred to a new shield. A design sheet may depict the proposed assembled shield without claiming it exists in the current inventory; label that sheet accordingly.

## A8. Runtime, information and revision interfaces

1. Identify whether the request is a current scene, historical scene, character reference or design proposal.
2. Read its applicable game state and approved references. Preserve source/approval distinctions and record missing essential references.
3. Reconcile identity, age, condition, design revision, count and wearer-relative orientation before production.
4. Apply the requested revision in **every affected view**, preserving unrelated accepted features.
5. Inspect likeness, anatomy, attachments, lettering, heraldry and scene continuity. Flag proposals and unresolved conflicts.
6. Present the result with its proper status. Approval of art is not approval of new stats, fabrication, spending, PC action or a world-state correction.

Art reads committed events and permitted viewer knowledge. It cannot provide foreknowledge, expose hidden facts in an in-world map or invent Ian's response. Design discussion and re-rendering do not advance the game clock. Current wounds, repairs, possessions and status come from the scene's place in the story. Maps distinguish research/design views from character-known information; source images are not omniscient game records.
