# IRGS — The Hundred Years' War: Campaign Design Integration

Proposed Campaign Profile v0.3 · Character Creation, Mercenary Service, Independent Careers & Historical Entry Points

The attached Ten Primary Campaign Entry Points v0.2 provides the historical foundation for this update. It already distinguishes political conditions, technological capabilities, relative national strength, and faction-specific difficulty across ten historical starting dates.&#x20;

Pasted text.txt



The principal change is that players should no longer be expected to select a national faction as their character's permanent allegiance.

The Hundred Years' War must support individual lives occurring within the conflict, including people who do not participate in the war, people who fight for foreign powers, independent mercenaries, merchants serving several markets, and scholars whose discoveries alter the course of history.

The revised design should recognize four independent decisions:

1. When does the player enter the world? Choose a historical starting period.
2. Who does the player control? An existing character, a new character, or an eligible institutional perspective.
3. What is the character's background? National origin, family, upbringing, education, relationships, and life history.
4. What is the character doing? Independent living, civilian employment, military service, contracting, scientific work, or another pursuit.

None of these choices should automatically determine the others.

The ten starting periods remain part of one Grand Campaign, not ten separate games. A Scot's Tale remains a pre-designed character beginning within the 1415 Sub-Campaign, Last Push of the English.

The last historical entry, 1453, is not a campaign termination condition. A player may continue into the following centuries, experiencing social change, political developments, technological innovation, and new conflicts resulting from the simulation.

Historical accuracy establishes the selected world's initial conditions. Subsequent history is not scripted.

## 2. Expanded career and allegiance system

A character may live without serving any faction. They may also enter and leave multiple institutional relationships over their lifetime.

| Starting arrangement       | Example in the Hundred Years' War                                                 |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Independent civilian       | Farmer, traveller, artisan, merchant                                              |
| Permanent employment       | Steward serving a noble household                                                 |
| Military retinue           | Soldier retained by a lord or commander                                           |
| Individual mercenary       | Paid combatant accepting temporary service                                        |
| Free company               | Member or captain of an independently organized military company                  |
| Contracted professional    | Engineer, surgeon, interpreter, shipwright                                        |
| Foreign military service   | Scottish-born soldier serving English forces                                      |
| Institutional specialist   | Scholar, physician, administrator, military engineer                              |
| Multiple clients           | Merchant, contractor, or specialist with several employers                        |
| Unemployed or seeking work | Former soldier, displaced worker, newly trained apprentice                        |
| Faction-level control      | Player manages an eligible government or organization through authorized controls |

These should be arrangements constructed from reusable Core mechanisms, not permanent character classes.

A person may move between arrangements. A soldier might serve a noble, become unemployed following demobilization, take short-term contracts, and eventually establish a business.

### Mercenary service

Mercenary employment must be explicitly supported because the attached campaign catalogue identifies discharged soldiers, independent companies, garrison contracts, and paid military service as important features of several historical periods.&#x20;

Contracts should be governed by actual terms:

| Contract element | Simulation responsibility                                           |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Employer         | Which individual or institution hires the service                   |
| Contractor       | Individual, troop contingent, or independent company                |
| Assignment       | Garrison duty, expedition, escort, siege, protection, etc.          |
| Duration         | Specified term, campaign, task, or other termination condition      |
| Compensation     | Wages, provisions, advances, rewards, or other agreed consideration |
| Equipment        | Who supplies, owns, and maintains necessary equipment               |
| Command          | Which orders the contractor must obey                               |
| Exclusivity      | Whether concurrent contracts are permitted                          |
| Liabilities      | Debts, unmet obligations, breaches, and disputed terms              |
| Termination      | Completion, dismissal, expiration, negotiated release, or breach    |

Contracts establish employment obligations; they do not necessarily establish permanent nationality, citizenship, or political allegiance.

An autonomous mercenary company should remain a persistent organization with its own roster, leadership, equipment, finances, supplies, agreements, and history.

A contract's expiry should not cause its members or equipment to disappear.

The following are proposed expansions to each existing Sub-Campaign, not replacements for the historical research.

### Specific historical integration requirements

1337 — The War Begins

Scottish origin must not automatically assign a character to the Bruce cause, Balliol supporters, or a French military organization. These are separate political interests with their own circumstances. An individual may support one, oppose one, avoid the conflict, or seek service elsewhere.

1360 — Peace of Brétigny

This period should strongly support mercenary and veteran careers. The campaign's existing description explicitly identifies discharged soldiers and armed companies as an important consequence of the peace settlement. Such actors must continue to exist after their former employment ends.&#x20;

Pasted text.txt



1369 — Caroline War

Gascon, Breton, Castilian, French, and English organizations should be modeled separately. A Gascon noble's legal obligations, territorial interests, and personal service relationships cannot be inferred solely from a simplified English/French faction choice.

1389 — Long Truce

A player must be able to establish an entirely civilian livelihood. The truce creates opportunities for trade, court service, restoration, professional development, and intergenerational advancement. Mercenaries may seek other work, maintain contracts, or attempt to find new employers.&#x20;

Pasted text.txt



1415 — English Invasion

This is the most immediately relevant period for A Scot's Tale.

The campaign should distinguish English royal forces, the French royal administration and Armagnac interests, Burgundian political networks, Scottish institutions, Breton interests, Gascon institutions, and independent military contractors.

Burgundy must not be represented as an automatic English ally in 1415. Its position differs from the later alliance of the 1420s.&#x20;

Pasted text.txt



A Scottish character wishing to serve Henry V should be evaluated individually, potentially considering sponsorship, recruitment practices, practical capability, existing obligations, location, and political circumstances.

1422 — Two Kings, One Crown

This period requires particular care because the two rival claimants to the French crown have distinct political administrations and institutional networks.

The Lancastrian government and Charles VII's Valois government should not be merged into a single selectable faction named France. Their territorial control, relationships, claims, and recruitment opportunities differ.&#x20;

Pasted text.txt



1435 — Burgundian Reversal

The Treaty of Arras changes Burgundy's diplomatic position. It must not automatically convert every Burgundian soldier, household servant, garrison contractor, or merchant into a direct employee of Charles VII.

Existing contracts and loyalties remain individually accounted for.

1444 — Last Truce

This is particularly suitable for a civilian or institutional career campaign.

Military reformers, scholars, craftsmen, administrators, and former soldiers should be viable protagonists. A player may help develop a new military institution or retain a peaceful professional livelihood.

The historical ordonnance companies of 1445 and franc-archer arrangements of 1448 must not be automatically present in the 1444 initial state. Nor should their later creation be guaranteed.&#x20;

Pasted text.txt



1449 — Final Campaigns

Professional artillery and engineering careers become especially relevant.

However, being a military engineer or artillerist requires actual expertise, personnel, materials, equipment, and employment or patronage. Merely selecting that occupation must not manufacture a complete artillery establishment.

1453 — Aftermath

This remains a fully playable starting period.

Players should be able to pursue postwar reconstruction, property disputes, political employment, commercial growth, mercenary work, and entirely unrelated careers.

The campaign must not force the Wars of the Roses, renewed Anglo-French warfare, or any other later event. The attached profile explicitly treats 1453 as the conventional war's conclusion rather than an obligatory end to subsequent developments.&#x20;

## 5. Revised campaign character-selection interface

The website should no longer present nationality and military faction as two mandatory choices in one character-creation stage.

Instead, the starting interface should ask separately who the character is, what they have experienced, and which institutions they currently belong to or want to approach.

### Proposed selection flow

Begin without a current employer or faction service obligation. Pursue opportunities during gameplay.

Interface prototype. These options are starting arrangements, not permanent classes or guaranteed employment.

### Following a service-related choice

The player should be asked whether their character:

- Already holds an established position.
- Has received an employment or service offer.
- Is actively applying or seeking contracts.
- Has completed previous service but is currently independent.
- Belongs to an independent company or professional organization.
- Wishes to leave this decision until gameplay.

Only then should the website offer relevant organizations from the date-specific world registry.

For example, a Scottish character starting in 1415 could browse potential English military employers without changing nationality.

The interface should make clear that available to approach and guaranteed to accept the character are different things.

A character could also have several simultaneous relationships: membership in a guild, employment by a noble household, and a separate commercial contract with another institution.

Whether those commitments can coexist depends on their actual terms.

## 6. Historical society and institutional simulation

One major adjustment to the existing campaign profile is necessary.

The historical database cannot contain only the principal national combatants.

It must support several organizational scales:

| Organizational scale                   | Examples                                                           |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Kingdoms and governments               | English crown, competing French royal governments, Scottish crown  |
| Territorial principalities             | Brittany, Burgundy, Gascon lordships                               |
| Political associations                 | Noble political networks and competing court interests             |
| Military organizations                 | Royal armies, garrisons, noble retinues, independent companies     |
| Economic institutions                  | Merchants, guilds, workshops, estates                              |
| Educational and scholarly institutions | Universities, schools, religious centres of learning               |
| Households                             | Noble families, farming households, commercial families            |
| Contracting groups                     | Mercenary companies, transport providers, construction specialists |

An organization can exist without participating in the war.

A faction can have internal divisions. Multiple organizations can serve the same crown while retaining different leadership, property, legal relationships, and operational autonomy.

### Characters and institutions must be separate

A noble family, for example, may possess estates, maintain a retinue, employ administrators, and establish relationships with the crown.

The family's soldiers do not automatically become direct royal employees merely because their lord owes service to the king.

Likewise, a company hired to transport English soldiers does not automatically become part of the English military.

The system should preserve actual contracts, employment relationships, command arrangements, property ownership, and political obligations.

This follows the existing catalogue's distinction between royal authority, effective administration, local cooperation, and the logistical requirements of military operations.&#x20;

## 7. Scientific development and alternative histories

Because the user can create characters with civilian, scholarly, engineering, or experimental backgrounds, the campaign must allow them to influence the development of technology.

A 1415 character could pursue mechanical engineering, metallurgy, medicine, agricultural methods, or other contemporary fields of knowledge.

For historical presentation, professions such as natural philosopher, scholar, master craftsman, engineer, or physician may be more appropriate than the modern occupational label scientist.

However, those historical labels must not constrain what the character can ultimately discover.

### Technological progression requirements

The campaign must distinguish:

1. Whether a technology is historically known at the selected starting date.
2. Whether its underlying principles are understood by particular actors.
3. Whether the necessary materials, tools, and production methods are available.
4. Whether a character can feasibly experiment with or develop it.
5. Whether the resulting discovery has been reproduced, manufactured, or disseminated.

An invention appearing centuries before its real historical date is permitted when the simulation's causal requirements have actually been satisfied.

Conversely, simply knowing the modern name of an invention must not grant the player character the knowledge or resources necessary to manufacture it.

This also means each of the ten starting periods should load its historically appropriate technological baseline, rather than imposing an absolute technological ceiling for all future years.

### Important temporal distinction

Faction and organization availability should be resolved from the selected date's world state.

New historical game: Load the corresponding historical initial conditions.

Ongoing divergent game: Use the actual state produced by the simulation.

A mercenary company created by a player in 1370 could still exist in 1415 if its organization survived. Conversely, a historically prominent organization might have ceased to exist in that playthrough.

The historical catalogue must not restore or erase organizations simply because the calendar reaches a known historical date.

### Migration implications

Existing campaign saves should not be automatically altered to match the revised design.

If existing character records combine nationality with national military allegiance, those fields need controlled migration that preserves their established meanings and any uncertainty.

Likewise, existing factions and mercenary companies need stable identities rather than being recreated whenever a different historical period is selected.

## 12. Recommended development sequence

The most logical development order for Codex is:

1. Campaign Registry: Represent the ten historical starting periods and their Sub-Campaign identities.
2. Temporal World Data: Support date-specific actors, institutions, political relationships, technology, and available organizational roles.
3. Character Creation Integration: Incorporate nationality, family, upbringing, training, relationships, and optional affiliations.
4. Service & Contract Interface: Enable independent characters, employment, mercenary service, applications, and contractual obligations through Core mechanisms.
5. Starting Scenario Manager: Configure AST and other pre-designed openings without duplicating campaign worlds.
6. Validation & Persistence: Verify starting-state consistency, player authority, accounting, and long-term world continuity.
7. Gameplay Integration: Connect character careers, institutional relationships, and historical development to the live simulation.

### Status and outstanding work

Established design requirements: Nationality is separate from faction service; affiliation is optional; independent and mercenary careers are valid; campaigns diverge from historical outcomes; character and institutional state persists across generations.

Proposed additions: The specific UI workflows, expanded scenario concepts, logical interface names, and period-specific career presentation.

Not yet completed: Historical-state database population, comprehensive actor and organization rosters, exact inventories and finances, Core 3.3 interface reconciliation, difficulty calibration, and operational testing.

The attached historical catalogue explicitly identifies the date-specific world-state database as its next implementation requirement. That work remains necessary before the website can provide fully authoritative start-date selections rather than descriptive scenarios.&#x20;
