# Larius: cultural foundations of the adopted setting

The world’s creator supplied these inspirations after the first expansion draft. They guide the adopted expanded setting under KIRA-U06. They describe fictional Kira societies and selected design influences, not the transplantation of complete real-world histories or universal claims about real populations.

| Nation | Inspirations and their roles | Confirmed identity in Kira |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sadion | Roman and Holy Roman design; legionary terminology and similar broad tactics; medieval Holy Roman equipment | A Sadire human realm whose Legion combines organized military traditions with medieval material culture. |
| Estru | Pre-unification medieval German political fragmentation; Etruscan and Carthaginian culture | Human descendants of the Sadire and a long-resident Desert Elven population helped form its culture; the nation is oppressed by larger and more powerful neighbours. |
| Akaria | Sasanian and Byzantine heavy cavalry; insular medieval England after William as a cultural reference | Industrious, aggressive, insular and xenophobic dwarven imperial culture, with pomp, arrogance and heavily armoured Battle Ram cavalry intended to break enemy lines. |
| Everwood / Evalia | French-influenced cultural prestige without an accent caricature | An isolationist, superiorist elven republic, still predominant in magic, with numerous mingling sub-races and a caste system that republican government has not abolished. |
| Kyrinus | Medieval Italian mercantile societies | A more accepting and humble Karthinian elven society with a trader’s outlook. |

Estru receives the Carthaginian cultural influence. The Karthinians of Kyrinus receive the medieval Italian mercantile influence; the similarity of their name is not a reason to exchange those inspirations.

## Everwood’s caste relationships

Sea elves hold the highest caste because of their retained magical superiority within that social tradition. The creator’s subsequent clarification establishes that Ice Elven magic exceeds even Sea Elven magic. Highest recognized caste in Evalia and greatest magical capability among all elven peoples are therefore distinct claims. Plains and Tree elves are the most numerous and constitute the majority of society. Sea elves feel superior to them but recognize them as equals. Tree elves correspond to the Forest terminology used in the regional peoples list and the Tree terminology in the older history.

This preserves a distinction between highest magical prestige and recognition of peers. It is not a simple ruling Sea caste above an undifferentiated Plains-and-Tree underclass.

Mountain elves are the least numerous. Their strength makes them efficient soldiers against Humans and Dwarves, but weaker magical capability leaves them looked down upon. The precise caste ranks of Ice and Desert elves, the classification of mixed-sub-race households and the exact legal consequences remain open; their cultural, magical and geographical identities are developed below.

The society can value a Mountain elf’s military service while withholding corresponding social respect. It can recognize a Plains elf such as Kieran as a peer while a Sea elf remains convinced of ancestral superiority. The republic does not remove those contradictions.

## Desert Elves and Estru

Desert Elves are long-resident inhabitants of the lands that became Estru. Their initial participation in the history of Sadire settlement was unwilling, but centuries have passed and they helped form Estru culturally. Estru’s human population descends from the Sadire; its culture also owes a foundational contribution to Desert Elves. The creator has not specified the original coercive arrangements, a first-settlement date or present legal status.

Desert Elven communities also live in Kyrinus and Evalia. Elven ancestry, civic identity and national allegiance need not coincide. Their role in Estru does not turn every Desert Elf into an Estrusian citizen, or every Estrusian into a human.

## Ice Elves, magic and suitable habitat

Ice Elves possess superior magic even to Sea Elves. They mature faster and live slightly less long. No maturity age or revised numerical lifespan is supplied; the old list’s Ice Elven reference of 870 years and Sea Elven reference of 900 years remain unchanged.

They are primarily isolationists because most of Larius is unsuitable for their habitation and dwarves hold dominion over the suitable region. Their isolation consequently has an ecological and political basis. Their greater magic does not itself supply territorial control, a sixth nation or the highest Evalian caste. Exact settlement boundaries, leadership, agreements with Akaria and the history of dwarven dominion remain undeveloped.

These confirmations create room for stories about residence, permission and access without establishing a past expulsion or an inevitable war. Shelter customs and civic religious exchange in the lorebook belong to the adopted setting. Possible negotiations retain open outcomes.

## Dominant peoples and other inhabitants

The creator confirms that the original peoples are the dominant races of Larius and the leaders of their respective nations. Other minor races exist with varying intelligence and sentience, beyond ordinary flora and fauna. The old regional list is therefore not an exhaustive census or species catalogue.

Sivren, Keld and Vesh belong to the adopted expanded setting. Their names originated in the expansion rather than the old documents. The Vesh’s precise individual capacities remain undeveloped; the named peoples do not constitute an exhaustive continental census.

## Adoption of the expanded setting

The framework above comes from creator statements KIRA-U03, KIRA-U04 and KIRA-U05 in `../source_manifest.json`. The structured version is `national_inspirations.json`, including its `elven_communities` records. The exact original wording is retained in the statements, including the distinction between source spelling and normalized historical labels.

KIRA-U06 adopts the expanded lore as working canon, including the Iron Lances, religious traditions, social customs, institutions, supporting cast and Kieran’s characterization. Their origin in new writing remains part of the creative history. The older national and character documents are preserved as a superseded archive and do not constrain current development.
