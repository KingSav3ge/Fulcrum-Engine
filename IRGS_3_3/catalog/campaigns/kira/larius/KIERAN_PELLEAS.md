# Kieran Pelleas: The Man Behind the Ages

Central character dossier · adopted Larius canon · reference present: 1342 A.A.

Kieran Pelleas is Larius’s main character. He is the continent’s recurring protagonist and the figure through whom its long history becomes personal. This role was specified by the world’s creator. It does not designate a player avatar, guarantee his survival or make him the sole person whose choices matter.

## 1. The man and the symbol

Kieran was born in Forlathis in 1000 B.A., at the close of the Evalian civil war. His father was the first prime minister of the republic; his mother was the daughter of the dethroned king. At 2342, he has outlived the founding crisis without escaping its meaning.

His snow-white hair, height, quiet manner and white Evalian stallion make him recognizable before anyone hears his name. He carries two steel sabers, an Evalian-wood bow, leather clothing reinforced with mithral and the practical tools of a traveller. He should look like someone who maintains useful things rather than someone who acquires a new sacred weapon each time the story changes direction.

The public sees an ancient ranger, a possible prince, an extraordinary mage and a witness who has survived what states prefer to call settled history. Those identities can be used against one another. Asking for his blessing can be an attempt to purchase legitimacy. Asking him to remember can be a way of demanding that he publicly accuse a friend.

Kieran’s own sense of himself is less grand. He measures a day by whether people who depended on him came home, whether an apprentice learned something useful and whether he can stand by the terms under which he accepted work. He notices the damaged stitching on a cloak before the embroidery on a minister’s robe.

## 2. What he wants, and what he has yet to learn

His immediate desire is to protect the people in his care without becoming the instrument of another preventable war. His deeper need is to accept that protection cannot mean possession of everyone else’s choices.

He is brave, nurturing and genuinely capable of care, as his actions and relationships show. His experience can nevertheless become a form of control. He may withhold a painful detail because he fears what someone will do with it. He may assign himself a risk that another person was entitled to choose. He can mistake the ability to endure another loss for permission to decide who must be spared it.

His guilt over the scout’s arrow is the centre of this pattern. The failure was real: he did not stop a member of his command from turning a fraught encounter into violence. But guilt becomes misleading when he treats it as an explanation for every decision made by every state afterward. Blaming himself for everything also places him at the centre of everything. Learning to recognize other people’s responsibility is part of learning to respect their agency.

The question that follows him is: “Can I help build something that does not need me to hold it together?” He has led the Rangers for centuries. He has had ample reason to postpone answering.

### The inheritance he does not stop carrying

Everwood’s confirmed caste order sharpens his position. He is a Plains elf, belonging to one of the numerous groups that Sea elves recognize as equals despite their own sense of magical superiority. His royal maternal lineage and republican paternal lineage give him additional prestige. His exceptional magic lets him enter settings in which a less favoured elf may be praised for useful service and denied comparable standing.

Mountain elves make the problem particularly visible: their strength is valued against Human and Dwarven enemies while weaker magic becomes an excuse to look down on them. Kieran can defend elves fiercely and still have learned assumptions about which elves should lead. His character need not begin as a modern egalitarian untouched by his upbringing. What he notices, excuses, resists or chooses to unlearn belongs to his continuing story.

The Rangers’ mix of elven ancestries can expose those assumptions in practical terms. If a Mountain Ranger is trusted to hold a line, is that same Ranger trusted to help set the company’s policy? Recognizing skill only where it serves an inherited hierarchy can become one of Kieran’s own failures, not merely an offence he identifies in other people.

The Desert Elves complicate any account in which he treats Estru simply as Sadire descendants needing an elven intermediary. They helped form that society themselves. He must learn whom a local assembly has authorized to speak, rather than assuming an elven household will welcome him as its natural representative. Their history is not merely a lesson arranged for his benefit.

The Ice Elves challenge a different assumption: magical distinction does not require recognition by Evalia or by Kieran. Their superior magical inheritance does not rank every individual above him, but it denies his homeland an uncomplicated claim to be the measure of all elven achievement. A proposed story could put his friendship with Cathius alongside an Ice Elven request concerning viable habitat. He can help secure a hearing without owning the settlement, speaking for the community or proving his concern through a spectacular spell.

The existence of other minor peoples adds a wider test. A famous negotiator between the five dominant societies may still overlook people those states do not treat as parties to an agreement. His refugee rescue remains a real achievement. It need not prove that he has already learned to hear every kind of voice.

## 3. His long life

Kieran is 2342 years old. His exceptional healing knowledge has prolonged his life far beyond the ordinary Plains Elven reference span of about 1000 years. Plains and Flatland are names for the same elven group; its ordinary lifespan is a reference span rather than an inviolable biological maximum.

The practice is called the Long Renewal. It is sustained personal healing and painstaking study of his own changing body, not a resurrection guarantee or a divine exemption from death. Wounds, illness, failed treatment and deliberate violence can still kill him. The precise method, reproducibility and supernatural costs remain undeveloped authoring questions; no new executable ability is granted by this explanation.

Renewing a body does not restore every lost memory, preserve every relationship or make centuries emotionally interchangeable. He can remember an old battlefield’s smell and fail to recall a friend’s laugh. He keeps written recollections partly because his confidence in a memory is not proof that it is complete.

People interpret his survival according to their own needs. A Turning Bough teacher may see an unfinished duty. A Continuance researcher sees evidence worth studying. A royalist may call it a sign that his bloodline must return. Kieran dislikes the last interpretation, but dislike does not prevent it from spreading.

## 4. The Ranger before the legend

His mother taught him the bow, his father taught him dual swordsmanship and a tutor introduced him to magic. He helped secure the lands that became Kyrinus and later commanded men in the frontier crisis with Akaria. His active service during the continental war spanned 228 years. He later withdrew to Vlaris, living under another name and learning skills that had little to do with public honour. The continental conflict itself lasted from 80 to 400 A.A.; the personal span is not the length of the whole war.

His hundred years of withdrawal were a period of changed work and ordinary relationships. Someone who knew him then may remember a competent leatherworker with an irritating habit of repairing other people’s tools without being asked. They need not have understood his lineage or the extent of his power.

The evacuation during the rebellions of 500 A.A. Brought him back into a different kind of responsibility. The people he helped escape included Karthinians, Estrusians, Akarians, Evalians and Sadire. The early Rangers grew from people who had chosen one another while their states demanded separation.

Their present elven-only membership is therefore not an incidental recruitment rule. It is a contradiction inside the company’s memory. Kieran may have reasons for preserving it; he also has reasons to question whether the institution has remembered its founders honestly. The setting should let that question hurt without assigning him a ready-made answer.

## 5. Faith, death and the use of power

Kieran respects practices that make people responsible to one another. His personal religious life draws most closely on the Turning Bough, especially its insistence that memory should nourish the living rather than govern them forever. He is not its chosen messiah, high priest or final authority.

He keeps private observances for people he could not save. Publicly reading their names is difficult because listeners often want to turn confession into proof of virtue. He would prefer that the dead be remembered as people rather than as a measure of his suffering.

His relationship with restoration is similarly uncomfortable. His study of resurrection and raising the dead arises partly from compassion and partly from his inability to bear an old failure. He can do good with knowledge acquired for a troubling reason. He can also be tempted to cross a boundary because refusing feels too much like abandoning someone again.

He rejects the proposition that a person restored to life owes obedience to the restorer. He also knows that gratitude can become dependence without anyone stating the bargain aloud. The Last Door fellowship can challenge him precisely where his good intentions are strongest.

## 6. The people who can reach him

Cathius Akarian is a personal friend. Their friendship lets Kieran see a dwarven ruler as a man who has made choices within an aggressive, insular and often xenophobic imperial society. It also gives both men grounds for uncomfortable exceptions: an Akarian courtier can admire Kieran without respecting elves generally, and Kieran can respect Cathius while understating the costs Akaria imposes on weaker neighbours. Cathius values Kieran’s honesty and sometimes grows impatient with his habit of making a difficult decision sound like a responsibility he must bear alone. Kieran, in turn, sees the danger in an emperor assuming that reforms sensible to him will arrive harmlessly everywhere his goods are sold.

Lord Eric Vornwolf is also a friend. His exact domain remains unassigned in the current setting. He can speak to Kieran outside institutional performance; his wider realm or office remains to be developed. Their private disagreements can be authored once his wider place in the setting is established.

Everwood’s officials need his testimony and worry about his symbolic authority. The more accepting, mercantile society of Kyrinus can respect his wartime courage while exposing the condescension he has learned to disguise as patient advice. The Karthinian Council can reject any suggestion that he remains entitled to supervise its people. Ordinary Rangers know the habits the public legend misses: when he becomes quiet for the wrong reason, when he has not slept and when he is preparing to leave a dangerous task to himself.

He needs relationships in which correction does not become betrayal. Without them, longevity can turn the company of younger people into an endless procession of dependants who are never allowed to become equals.

## 7. How to write him in a scene

Kieran speaks plainly when someone needs a practical answer. He becomes guarded when asked what a failure meant to him. He asks names and remembers tasks. He gives a nervous person something useful to do instead of making reassurance into a performance.

He is not humourless. His humour is dry, occasionally badly timed and often directed at the difference between a grand description and a familiar inconvenience. When comfortable, he enjoys good work, ordinary meals and the quiet satisfaction of teaching someone a repair that will last.

His defensiveness about insults against elves is an inherited trait worth retaining as a flaw. It can reveal where generosity stops being as even-handed as he believes. A human or dwarf who has earned his respect can challenge that inconsistency without being treated by the narrative as insolent for noticing it.

When angry he tends toward precision. He may become dangerously certain before he becomes loud. When afraid of losing someone he can offer a reasonable-sounding plan whose real purpose is to remove their choice. Neither tendency erases his care; both make it a source of conflict.

Sample dialogue: “You can ask me what I remember. You cannot ask my memory to make the choice for you.”

Sample dialogue: “The contract says who pays us. It does not decide who we are allowed to leave behind.”

Sample dialogue: “If the Rangers cannot tell me I am wrong, I have built a court and called it a company.”

## 8. His present story

The central arc is called The Price of Peace in this edition. The expedition offer, contested historical records, the Rangers’ future and the politics of restoration all ask versions of the same question: can Kieran help people move beyond the settlements that shaped him without deciding their future on their behalf?

He may support a voyage, expose an injustice, defend a friend, refuse a profitable commission or discover that his first interpretation was wrong. None of those outcomes is scheduled. His centrality comes from the weight of his connections and choices.

Other characters can accomplish things he cannot, challenge his priorities, defeat his plans and carry a story beyond his reach. If he dies, the continent loses a central person and must live with the consequences. It does not become an invalid campaign world.

## Canon and authorship

This dossier is adopted setting canon under KIRA-U06. Kieran’s Long Renewal, personal faith, habits, flaws, relationships and central arc are part of the current characterization. The earlier character sheet is a superseded creative archive. Later creator directions can revise this characterization without needing agreement from an older document.

The Price of Peace presents an ongoing conflict with open outcomes. Kieran’s narrative centrality supplies no guaranteed survival, automatic authority or player selection.
